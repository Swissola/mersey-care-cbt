## Part 1 — Maintaining Gains & Relapse Prevention

### Early Warning Signs


Remaining well after a course of CBT is not about never struggling again — it is about recognising early signs quickly and responding before things escalate.

Early warning signs are subtle changes that can easily go unnoticed. They do not mean you are severely depressed or anxious again — they are a signal that things have started to drift, and that it is time to act.

**To identify your early warning signs:**

Return to your 5 Areas Model from Session 1. The symptoms you recorded at the start of the course are your guide. Changes in those same areas are likely to be your early warning signs.

Ask yourself:
- What does my body do when things start to slip?
- What thoughts tend to appear early on?
- What do I start to do (or stop doing) behaviourally?
- What emotions tend to show up first?

## Part 2 — Lifestyle & Future

### Healthy Lifestyle


Several daily habits have good evidence for supporting mental health. These are not a replacement for therapy — but they complement it meaningfully.

**Diet**
What you eat and drink influences your mood. A balanced, varied diet helps maintain stable energy and mood. General principles:
- Reduce saturated fat
- Reduce refined sugar and switch to low-calorie drinks
- Reduce salt
- Increase fibre — wholegrains, vegetables, legumes

**Exercise**
Exercise consistently shows one of the strongest effects on mood of any lifestyle factor. Even 30 minutes of moderate activity (brisk walking, cycling) three times a week can significantly reduce symptoms of both anxiety and depression. If you haven't exercised in a while, build up gradually — don't overdo it early on.

**Pacing your life**
If life feels like a constant rush, it is worth reviewing what is driving that. Make space for hobbies, rest, and things you genuinely enjoy. This is not indulgent — it is protective.

**Sleep**
Poor sleep and low mood reinforce each other — each makes the other worse. The habits covered in Session 4 are worth maintaining long term: a consistent bedtime, keeping the bedroom for sleep, avoiding caffeine late in the day, and using the 20-minute rule if you can't sleep. If sleep starts to slip, it is often one of the earliest signs that things are drifting.

**Talking**
Keeping thoughts and feelings entirely to yourself creates internal pressure. Sharing with someone you trust — whether a friend, family member, or professional — helps release that pressure and provides perspective.

**Relationships**
Friendships and supportive relationships provide a buffer against anxiety and depression. They need to be maintained — and where there is a problem in a relationship, addressing it sooner rather than later is almost always better.

### Coping Statements


Coping statements are short phrases that interrupt anxious thoughts and replace them with more realistic ones. With enough practice, they become automatic.

**When you notice anxiety rising — say STOP, then use one of these:**

*When anxiety is near:*
- "I'm going to be all right. My feelings are not always rational. I just need to relax and calm down."
- "My anxiety will not kill me. It is not dangerous — just uncomfortable. I'll be fine."
- "I know this feeling. I'm anxious again. That's OK — it will pass. I'll take a few deep breaths."

*When preparing for a difficult situation:*
- "I've done this before, so I know I can do it again."
- "When this is over, I'll be glad I did it."
- "I know this seems hard now, but it will become easier over time."

*When feeling overwhelmed:*
- "I can focus on what I'm doing even while feeling anxious. If I stay focused, the anxiety will reduce."
- "I feel overwhelmed, but I'm not going to give in. I'll keep going and focus away from the problem."

### Keys to Success


- **Be patient.** Change takes time. CBT skills deepen with practice, not overnight.
- **Take small, gradual steps.** Gradual, consistent progress is more sustainable than sudden effort.
- **Recognise your improvements.** Even small ones. Give yourself credit.
- **Setbacks are part of recovery.** They are not failure — they are information. Use them.
- **The past does not equal the future.** Previous patterns can be changed with consistent practice.
