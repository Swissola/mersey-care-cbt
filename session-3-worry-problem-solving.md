## Part 1 — Dealing with Worry & Grounding

### When Worry Becomes a Problem

Worrying is something all human beings experience. It becomes a problem when it is:

- **Excessive** — out of proportion to the actual situation
- **Uncontrollable** — you cannot stop it even when you try
- **Intrusive** — it interrupts daily life and concentration
- **Persistent** — it seems to always be present and causes significant distress

**Physical symptoms worry can cause:**

- Feeling restless, on edge, unable to relax
- Physical tension — tightness or stiffness in muscles
- Sleep disturbance — difficulty falling or staying asleep
- Problems concentrating or getting things done
- Avoidance of triggers that increase the worry

---

### Worry Time Technique

This technique works by containing worry to a set period, rather than letting it run throughout the day.

**Step 1 — Create a worry period**
- Choose a specific time each day (e.g. 5pm, before making tea)
- Make it a comfortable time, free from distraction
- Allow a set amount of time — 20 to 30 minutes is typical

**Step 2 — Postpone worries during the day**
- As soon as a worry appears, acknowledge it briefly and postpone it
- Note it in a couple of words in a small notebook
- Remind yourself: *"I'll think about this at worry time. I don't need to deal with it now."*
- Bring your attention back to the present

**Step 3 — Return to worries at the designated time**
- At your worry period, review your list
- If a worry no longer seems relevant — no further action needed
- If a worry still needs attention, spend no more than your set time on it

> 💡 *You will often find that many of your postponed worries feel much less significant — or have resolved themselves — by the time worry time arrives.*

---

### Grounding Yourself in the Present

Anxiety focuses the mind on a future that hasn't happened. Grounding techniques bring you back to the present moment in your body and your surroundings.

**Techniques to try:**

- **Name yourself in the moment** — say your name, your age, where you are, what you've done today, what you'll do next. *"My name is ________, I am __ years old. I am in my living room in [town]. I woke up at __ today..."*

- **Clothing and body awareness** — notice the sensation of your clothes, the weight of your feet on the floor, the texture of what you're sitting on.

- **Sound scan** — stop and listen. Name the sounds you can hear nearby, then gradually move your awareness outward to more distant sounds.

- **Object focus** — pick up an object and focus on it with all five senses. Notice how it looks, feels, sounds, smells. Don't label or judge — just notice.

- **Walk deliberately** — get up and take slow steps, paying attention to each one.

- **Tend to plants or soil** — physical contact with plants and earth is an excellent natural grounder.

## Part 2 — Problem Solving & PMR

### The 7-Step Problem Solving Framework

Problems are part of life. Anxiety and depression can both cause and be caused by unresolved problems. This 7-step framework gives you a structured way to work through them.

**Step 1 — Identify the problem**
Keep a diary of your day, rating anxiety from 0–10 at different points. After a week, look for patterns to identify your key problems. List them and put the most anxiety-provoking at the top.

**Step 2 — Identify possible solutions**
Brainstorm as many potential solutions as possible. At this stage, nothing is rejected — no matter how unhelpful, silly, or impractical it seems. Quantity matters here.

**Step 3 — Analyse strengths and weaknesses**
For each solution, consider its advantages and disadvantages — likelihood of success, resources needed, possible consequences.

**Step 4 — Select a solution**
Choose the best option based on your analysis. Consider what resources (time, money, support) you'll need.

**Step 5 — Plan implementation**
Break the solution into steps. Consider: what, where, when, and with whom.

**Step 6 — Do it**
Put your plan into action. Can you do it now? If not, schedule it and let the worry go until then.

**Step 7 — Review progress**
Praise yourself for the effort regardless of outcome. Record what happened. If it didn't work, what did you learn? Is there another solution from Step 2 worth trying?

### Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

Progressive Muscle Relaxation is a technique for achieving deep physical relaxation by systematically releasing tension from each part of the body in turn. It is particularly effective for the muscle tension that accompanies chronic worry and anxiety.

#### PMR Script

> 📝 *Sit in a comfortable chair or lie down. Ensure you won't be disturbed. Allow around 15–20 minutes.*

- [ ] Close your eyes. Feel the chair or floor supporting your whole body. Take 2 slow, deep breaths and let tension begin to flow out.

- [ ] **Head and forehead** — notice how your forehead feels. Let any tension go; feel your forehead become smooth and wide. Release tension around your eyes, mouth, cheeks and jaw. Let your teeth part slightly.

- [ ] **Neck** — let the chair take the weight of your head. Feel your neck relax. Your head is becoming heavy and floppy. Let your shoulders lower gently down. Your neck is longer, your shoulders are wider.

- [ ] **Arms and hands** — let them sink down into the chair. They are becoming heavy and limp.

- [ ] **Back** — from your neck to your hips. Let the tension go and feel yourself sinking into the chair.

- [ ] **Hips, legs and feet** — let them relax and roll slightly outwards. Notice the feeling of relaxation taking over.

- [ ] **Breathing** — notice your abdomen gently rising and falling. Let your next breath be a little deeper, a little slower.

- [ ] Rest here, breathing slowly and rhythmically.

- [ ] When ready, count back from 5 to 1. Open your eyes. Wiggle fingers and toes, breathe deeply and stretch. Pause before rising.

> 💡 *With regular practice (aim for daily), your body learns to reach this state more quickly and more deeply.*
